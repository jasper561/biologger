How these numbers are worked out

Cell. ER14505M, 1800 mAh nominal at +20 °C. Capacity is derated to 92% at 0 °C and 65% at −40 °C. The M is the spiral-wound cell — a bobbin ER14505 would not survive the WiFi pulses at all, let alone the cold.

Per sample. ~1.2 s awake at ~45 mA — about 0.3 s to boot out of deep sleep, then the DS18B20, whose 12-bit conversion takes 750 ms with the part powered throughout. That one sensor is most of the cost of taking a sample.

Idle. ESP32 deep sleep is ~10 µA and the ADXL362 keeps watching for motion on ~3 µA. But the MAX31855 draws ~900 µA continuously whenever it is powered, so unless a GPIO power-gate is fitted that single part dominates the entire idle budget — tick the box above to see what it costs you. With the gate, idle falls to about 15 µA for the whole board.

Shake-checks. Each shake opens a 120 s Bluetooth advertising window at ~30 mA, so roughly 1 mAh per check. Set how often you expect to pick the logger up; it is a small term unless you check it many times a day.

Deep sleep is implemented (firmware 3.2.0) but is off until you send SLEEP:ON, so it does not get in the way of bench testing. With sleep off the logger stays awake and advertising continuously, which is closer to 1.5 days regardless of interval — these figures describe the deployed state, with sleep on.

Storage is exact, not modelled: it comes from the logger's real filesystem size and its 23-byte record format.

Deep sleep & shake-to-wake

With sleep off the logger stays awake and advertising, which is convenient on the bench but flattens the cell in about a day and a half. With sleep on it wakes only to sample — and becomes findable again when you shake it.

Deep sleep between samples
Required for any deployment lasting more than a couple of days. While asleep the logger does not advertise, so shaking it is how you get back in.
Test the shake before you seal anything. Once sleep is on and USB is gone, a shake is the only way to reach the logger. Confirm it works: turn sleep on, unplug USB, wait past one sample interval, check that it will not connect, then shake it and check that it will. A live USB console deliberately keeps the logger awake, so sleep behaviour is invisible while it is plugged in.

Connecting to the sensor (Bluetooth)

To control the logger and view live data, connect to it over Bluetooth using this page first.

Desktop / laptop
  1. Open a modern browser — Chrome or Edge are recommended.
  2. Go to jasper561.github.io/biologger.
  3. Click Connect via Bluetooth above and select the logger from the popup menu.
iPhone

Apple's default browsers — Safari and Chrome for iOS — block the Bluetooth features this page needs, so iPhone needs a browser built for it instead.

  1. Download the Bluefy app from the App Store — a browser that supports Bluetooth.
  2. The easy way: tap your iPhone against the right side of the device. The built-in NFC tag automatically opens Bluefy and loads the correct URL.
  1. The manual way: open the Bluefy app and navigate to jasper561.github.io/biologger.
  2. Tap connect and select the device.
Downloading your data (WiFi)

While a small log can be downloaded over Bluetooth, downloading a large CSV file is much faster over WiFi.

  1. Connect to the sensor over Bluetooth using the steps above.
  2. Open the Advanced tab.
  3. Scroll down to the WiFi section and enter the credentials for your local WiFi network — your phone or computer must be on the same network.
  4. Click Connect & save.
  5. Once connected, the app generates an IP download link.
Because of browser security rules, clicking the link directly might not work. Instead, copy the IP link, open a new browser tab, and paste it into the address bar. The CSV file will download automatically.
Starting & deploying the logger

Before placing the logger into the field:

  1. Connect to the device over Bluetooth.
  2. Go to the Monitor tab.
  3. Click Start Logging.

To stop a deployment, connect over Bluetooth and click Stop Logging.

Live stream & bench testing

Verify that all sensors — thermocouple, ambient temperature, and accelerometer — are working correctly before deployment.

Single Reading. Click Single Reading on the Monitor tab for an instant snapshot.

Start Live Plot. Streams sensor readings in real time (~1 Hz). The stream automatically shuts off after 60 seconds to save battery.

Battery management & tracking

The logger actively tracks cell usage so you don't have to guess remaining runtime.

Viewing battery status. Go to Advanced → Battery to view total elapsed time, active logging hours, sample counts, and estimated percentage used.

Changing the cell. When you install a fresh battery, connect over Bluetooth and click I fitted a fresh battery. This resets the tracking counters so runtime estimates remain accurate for the new cell.
